The question

Which product line should the superstore grow next year to raise profit margin? A good answer must survive data cleaning, hold in every year, and not depend on mislabeled rows.

Finding 1 · The leader changes once impossible rows are removed

Margin = total profit ÷ total sales per sub-category. Grey dot = raw file; dark dot = after removing the 72 impossible orders (65 with Profit > Sales, 7 with losses larger than the sale). Phones falls from #1 (36.2%) to #3; Paper rises from #4 to #1. Overall margin drops from 29.0% to 22.7%.

The trap · Category labels are scrambled

Order counts by Category × Sub-category. Every sub-category appears under every category — Bookcases under Technology, Phones under Furniture. No cell is empty; only 32% of rows fit the standard taxonomy, about what random labels give (33%). Any ranking by Category (e.g. "Office Supplies earns 31.4%") is ranking noise.

What I changed: drop any label that fails a consistency check, re-run every headline without impossible rows, check that profit tracks sales, and require a leader to stay top 5 every year. When pushed, v2 shuffled the product labels 1,000 times: if all 12 lines were truly identical, a top margin of 30.3%+ would still appear 33% of the time, and some line would stay top 5 in all 3 years 67% of the time.

My call, as the human: the agent is right that the data cannot prove Paper is better, so I will not bet a year's growth budget on it. But shelf space gets allocated either way, and doing nothing is also a bet. Paper is the lowest-risk place to test: top 5 in every year and every region (only 18% of chance worlds produce that), in 96% of resamples, and least touched by bad rows. A small pilot is reversible and produces the clean data the agent says we need.

The one number I did not trust

"Office Supplies is the most profitable category at 31.4%." It is the obvious headline and my agent's first answer. It stacks a Category field that contradicts the products on top of 72 impossible orders. It shaped nothing in this decision.

Reviews · how far each batch was trusted

  • FakeReviews_Synthetic — excluded. All 100 texts are positive, yet 38 carry 1–2 stars; generator labels such as "(Tone: robotic)" are left in every review.
  • RestaurantReviews — directional only. Stars match the words, but 100 rows contain just 15 distinct sentences. Signal: service complaints dominate; 42 of 100 are 1–2 stars.
